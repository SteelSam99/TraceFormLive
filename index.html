Where did this claim come from?

We trace statements of fact back through the documentary record to their earliest verifiable source — and report plainly where that record runs out.

The work

A claim enters circulation, gets repeated, acquires citations, and becomes settled. Often the citations lead somewhere real. Sometimes they lead to another repetition, then another, and then to nothing at all.

A Trace follows that chain backward through archival holdings across multiple national and institutional collections, establishes where the claim first appears in a surviving document, and documents every link along the way — including the broken ones.

The output is a written report: the chain as found, the sources with their identifiers, and an explicit account of what could not be established.

Standards

  • Gaps are reported, not filled

    Where the record is silent, the report says so. A plausible reconstruction is not a finding.

  • Inference is labeled as inference

    Material that was retrieved and material that was reasoned to are marked separately and never blended.

  • No substitution of sources

    If a specified archive is unreachable or returns nothing, that is logged as a result. Nothing is quietly swapped in from elsewhere.

  • A partial retrieval yields a partial report

    Scope is set before the work begins and reported against honestly. Completeness is never implied.

  • Negative findings are findings

    "This claim has no traceable origin" is a result worth paying for, and it is delivered as readily as a confirmation.
