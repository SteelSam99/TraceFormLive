Here lies Salvino, son of Armato degli Armati of Florence, inventor of eyeglasses. May God forgive his sins.

Anno Domini MCCCXVII

No such epitaph was ever carved for such a man. The monument came down in 1923. The sentence is still in print.

What this traces

Spectacles were not invented by Salvino degli Armati. The attribution enters the printed record in Florence in 1738, is doubted in print by 1750, reaches English by 1823, is set in stone in 1855, is demolished by scholarship in 1920 — and is still being repeated in children's books in 1995.

This is not a history of spectacles. It is a history of a sentence, and of what happens to a sentence once enough people have repeated it.

The man in the record is real. He held civic office in Florence. What was attached to him was not his.

The chain

A solid line marks a node that names its source. After 1893 the line goes dotted: the claim keeps travelling, but it stops citing anything.

1684

Ferdinando Leopoldo del Migliore unverified

Firenze città nobilissima illustrata

Reported to be the first to claim an epitaph in Santa Maria Maggiore. Not retrieved, not confirmed, and it has not passed the instrument. Treated here as a hypothesis, not a link.

1738

Domenico Maria Manni prints the treatise

Firenze: Stamperia d'Anton-Maria Albizzini · three copies located

DEGLI OCCHIALI da naso INVENTATI DA SALVINO ARMATI GENTILUOMO FIORENTINO TRATTATO ISTORICO

Held by the Wellcome Library b30519706, the National Central Library of Florence bub_gb_PnM8I4dNvZ4C, and the Getty Research Institute degliocchialidan00mann. The first book on the history of spectacles.

1750

Doubted in print, twelve years later

Scelta di dissertazioni cavate da' più celebri autori, p. 238

Vera cosa è poi, che della persona di Salvino Armati non abbiamo memoria, fuorché il sapersi…

The claim was never uncontested. It was contested and propagated at the same time, in the same language, for the next 170 years.

1767

The real man, in a register of office-holders

Florentine prioristi, 1767 · 1779 · 1783

Michele di Cione Maffei. Salvino Armati. Matteo Benvenuti.

Three separate civic registers return the name with no mention of spectacles — one entry in a list, between two ordinary names. The fabrication did not invent a person. It attached an invention to a name already in the record, which is why arguing from absence could never dislodge it.

1823

Into English, in a Providence newspaper

The Rhode-Island American, and general advertiser, 25 November 1823, page 1

the introduction of sp6ctack*, which Salvino degli Armati first constructed about tlm year 1285.

Verified to the page. The damaged words are the scan's own — the phrase was found inside newspaper OCR this degraded, which is why no ordinary search reaches it. Note the date given: 1285, against 1284 and 1286 elsewhere. The year was never stable either.

1842

epitaffio». E in rubrica marginale: «Salvino Armati inventore degli occhiali, al parer…

Thirteen years before Villari's plaque. By this point the claim has been in Italian print for a century and in English for nineteen years.

1855

Villari sets it in stone

Plaque, Florence · removed 1923

Commonly named as the fabrication. It is not. It is the sixth node in a chain already 117 years old, and the monument records a claim rather than making one.

1893

Saturation

Eighteen newspapers, two continents, one year

May to December 1893

Papers with no editorial relationship, running the same item within months of each other. That signature is syndication — a wire filler moving on its own.

Elko, Nev. Cheyenne, Wyo. Lambertville, N.J. S. Londonderry, Vt. Worthington, Minn. Indianapolis, Ind. Deer Lodge, Mont. Deer Lodge, Mont. Penn's Grove, N.J. Toledo, Ohio (Ameryka, Polish) St. Johnsbury, Vt. River Falls, Wis. Anderson C.H., S.C. Hillsboro, Ohio Portland, Me. Wichita, Kan. Otago Witness, N.Z. West Coast Times, N.Z.

Before 1893 every appearance names a source — Manni, or an epitaph, or another book. After 1893 they stop. The claim keeps moving and the citation trail does not move with it. That is the step that cannot be taken back: not the fabrication, not the monument, but the year the sentence became something everyone already knew.

1920

Isidoro del Lungo takes it apart

Demolition

No other source credits Salvino. A fourteenth-century epitaph would read le peccata, not la peccata. And the word inventor did not exist in the Florentine vernacular of the period. A real Salvino degli Armati died in 1340 — a humble artisan who never dealt with eyeglasses.

1946

Still running, twenty-six years after

El Diario Palentino, Palencia, 10 and 11 January 1946 — consecutive days

atribuye la modalidad a un italiano llamado Salvino Armati, que los usó a principios del siglo…
1995

In a children's book, citing nothing

Incredible inventions · three editions of Grandpa's Glasses

engraved with gold lettering that read: "Here lies Salvino Armati, inventor of the eyeglass. God forgive him…"

Seventy-five years after the refutation, fifty-two after the monument came down. The surviving vector is books for children.

Still open

  1. 1684 is unverified. The del Migliore node rests on a single weak secondary source and has not been retrieved.
  2. The English vector is unexplained. Manni was never translated. Something carried the claim into English before 1823.
  3. The 1893 wire original has not been found. Locating it would date the saturation to a week rather than a year.
  4. The UK national archive returns nothing under this phrase, in a chain that otherwise spans four languages.
  5. Del Lungo's 1920 paper is not in the retrieved set. The demolition node is currently the only one still resting on secondary sources.
